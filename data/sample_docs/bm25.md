# BM25 and lexical retrieval

BM25 is a bag-of-words ranking function that scores a document by how often the
query terms appear in it, weighted by how rare those terms are across the corpus.
Decades after its introduction it remains a strong baseline that dense retrievers
do not reliably beat on their own.

## Term frequency saturation

Naive term frequency scoring rewards repetition without limit, so a document that
repeats a word fifty times scores fifty times higher than one that mentions it
once. BM25 instead saturates: the contribution of each additional occurrence
shrinks, controlled by the parameter k1, typically set between 1.2 and 2.0. The
intuition is that the tenth occurrence of a word tells you far less than the
second did.

## Inverse document frequency

A term that appears in nearly every document distinguishes nothing. Inverse
document frequency weights each term by the logarithm of the ratio between the
total number of documents and the number containing that term, so rare terms
dominate the score. This is why BM25 excels at identifiers, error codes,
acronyms and product names, the exact tokens a dense model is most likely to
blur into a nearby concept.

## Length normalization

Longer documents contain more words and would otherwise win by sheer volume.
BM25 divides by a factor derived from the document's length relative to the
average length in the corpus, tuned by the parameter b, conventionally 0.75.
Setting b to zero disables length normalisation entirely.

## The vocabulary mismatch problem

BM25's weakness is that it matches strings, not meaning. A query for "car" will
not match a document that only says "automobile", and no amount of parameter
tuning fixes that. This is precisely the failure mode dense retrieval addresses,
and precisely why combining the two recovers documents that either method alone
would miss.
